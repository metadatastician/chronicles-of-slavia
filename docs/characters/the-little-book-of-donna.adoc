== A note before you begin

This is a fan-style compendium of everything currently known about **Donna**,
one of the two player characters of _Chronicles of Slavia_. It is written from
inside the world — the way a devoted player might compile a character guide —
but every fact in it is drawn from the game's actual design canon
(`docs/design/*.md`), the Zone A game data (`engine/slavia-core/data/zone-a.sgs.toml`),
the engine source, and the playable prototype (`prototype/zone-a/index.html`).

Where the canon is genuinely settled, this book states it as settled. Where the
canon is still open — an unanswered question the designers themselves have
flagged — this book says so plainly, and collects those questions at the end
for whoever picks up the design next. Nothing here is invented lore dressed up
as fact.

_Chronicles of Slavia_ is presently a private, exploratory prototype (Level 1,
Zone A only — "Border Path"). This book describes a five-percent-built world.
Treat everything past Zone A as **design canon for later**, not shipped
content — this is called out chapter by chapter.

== Who She Is

Donna is a Bulgarian girl defined by structure — physical, emotional,
intellectual. She builds things, repairs things, organizes things. She is
**order, but order with compassion**.

That last clause matters more than it looks. This is not a game about a
"stern, orderly" character who exists to be a counterweight to a "wild, chaotic"
one. Donna's order is explicitly, canonically **protective** — never rigid for
its own sake. The design doctrine draws the line directly: her calm is not
passivity, it is a kind of quiet power. She can stabilize anything she
touches.

Underneath that competence sits a flaw the game is honest about: Donna
**carries too much responsibility, and refuses to ask for help**. She is the
one who fixes things, so she has learned — long before the Rift ever opened —
to be the one who is fixed, always, for everyone else. What she has never
quite learned is how to let someone hold *her* up.

[quote, docs/design/09-anya-and-donna-backstories.md]
Her order is not rigid — it is protective.

<<<

== Before the Rift

Donna grew up in a mountain village near the **Rhodopes** — the range that
runs along Bulgaria's southern border, famous in-world (and in life) for its
weaving and its mask-carving traditions. Her family were makers:

* Her **grandmother carved kukeri masks** — the ritual masks worn in Bulgarian
  folk tradition to drive out evil spirits.
* Her **uncle built wooden bridges**.

From them, Donna inherited two things at once: craftsmanship, and a
protective instinct. Long before any Rift, the village had already given her
a role — "**the reliable one**." She was the girl who fixed things, who
stayed calm, who didn't break, no matter what was asked of her.

But the design is careful to give this a cost. Inside, she felt trapped by
the *expectation* of it. She wanted to explore, to create, to be something
more than "the stable one" — a wish the story has not yet let her have, and
one that quietly defines who she needs to become across the Chronicles still
to come.

<<<

== The Day the World Broke

On the day of the Tear, Donna was doing exactly what the village expected of
her: **repairing a collapsed walkway**. Mid-repair, she paused at a strange
vibration running through the wood under her hands.

[quote, docs/design/09-anya-and-donna-backstories.md]
____
The mountains rumbled; the kukeri carvings glowed; the ground folded like
paper. She didn't fall because she slipped — she fell because the Rift opened
beneath her. It saw her order. It needed her order.
____

The wording is deliberate and worth sitting with: Donna did not stumble into
the story. The Rift did not take her by accident, the way a disaster might
take anyone standing nearby. It *saw* what she already was, and it *needed*
exactly that. Her order — her whole life's habit of holding things together —
is not incidental to the plot. It is the reason the plot has her in it at
all.

== Her Grammar — What "Settling" Actually Means

Donna's core gift is not a superpower bolted onto her from outside. In the
design's own words, her abilities are **emotional extensions** of who she
already was before the Tear — every one of them recognisable as "the girl who
holds everything together," now made literal.

=== Attunement, not command

Animals in Slavia are never tools, pets, buttons, or controllable units — they
are living participants, indicators, and sometimes helpers. Donna and Anya do
not *command* animals. They **change the rhythm of the world around them**.

[quote, docs/design/03-living-taxis.md]
Donna tends to calm, slow, settle, quieten, or reassure natural animals. The
animal's own nature determines the result.

Mechanically, what she moves is called **taxis** — the rate and direction of
a living thing's motion and response (the biological sense of the word: an
organism's taxis toward or away from a stimulus). **Donna lowers the taxis.**
She calms, settles, steadies, reassures — she draws out an animal's *peace*,
not its submission.

This is an important distinction the design insists on: Donna's gift works
**with** an animal's own nature, drawing out its genuine, best self. It never
imposes an alien state. A creature Donna has settled is still fully itself —
just at peace. That is categorically different from an enemy who forces
**Obedience** on a creature (mindless, will-less, an instrument). Donna's
settling and an enemy's obedience can look similar from a distance; the game
treats the difference as a moral line, not a cosmetic one.

There is a hard limit that holds no matter how skilled Donna becomes:
**attunement amplifies a thing's nature, it never perverts it.** She can calm
a creature; she cannot make it betray what it is.

=== An aura, not a button

The gift is described as an **aura** — it emanates from Donna and acts,
passively, on what passes near her, magnified and honed with practice (the
canon's own touchstone here is the Nen/Zen aura discipline from _Hunter ×
Hunter_: something you first leak unbidden, then learn to sense, contain, and
direct).

Mastery has two separate axes: **Strength** (how far and how powerfully the
aura reaches) and **Control** (how reliably and selectively it acts). Early
on, Donna's control is low and leaky — she can simply walk past a spot and
nothing happens, where Anya walking past that same spot startles a bird into
motion. That asymmetry is a deliberate feature of the early game, not a bug:
it is what makes the two girls feel different to *play*, not just to read
about.

=== Only Donna can stabilise

The taxis duality is not limited to animals — it extends to the world itself.

[quote, docs/design/06-attunement-and-modifiers.md]
Only Donna can stabilise; only Anya can agitate. Neither can do the other's
act.

The stream bridge in Zone A is the game's first and clearest demonstration of
this. Donna can still the crossing — settle its trembling, its instability —
so that it can be walked. Anya, trying the same thing, can only set it
**bucking and bullwhipping**: useless for crossing, though not useless for
everything (an agitated bridge might later deter a pursuer — a possibility
recorded, but not yet built, as the "Zone A climax idea: the marauder").

[quote, prototype/zone-a/index.html]
Donna stills the crossing. Now it can be crossed.

The line the game's own README uses to summarise the entire mechanic, in
miniature, is built from exactly this scene:

[quote, README.adoc]
Anya wants to cross. The bridge will not hold. Donna steadies it. Anya
crosses because Donna has made the path hold. This is the game in miniature.

=== What the world becomes without her

Together, Donna and Anya hold the world's taxis in balance — one lowering,
one raising. Apart, the balance breaks, and the world tips wholesale toward
whichever sister remains.

[quote, docs/design/06-attunement-and-modifiers.md]
Anya taken (only Donna's lowering is left): the world grows over-sedate,
lifeless — dulled and still — and stays that way, harder to rouse, until
Donna reaches her.

This is not flavour text. It is a standing gameplay pressure that persists
until the two are reunited — the world itself makes Donna's absence, and
Anya's, felt.

[[attire]]
== What She Wears

Donna's costume is culturally grounded — not an aesthetic layered on top of
her character, but the visual language of her whole nature. The design is
explicit about getting the specific folk-costume region right: Donna is
**Rhodope**, not Северняшка (Severnyashka, the two-apron costume of Northern
Bulgaria) — a distinction the design canon corrects deliberately, because the
Rhodopska nosia is "the sober, grounded one — the visual of stability," where
Severnyashka would have sent the wrong signal entirely.

=== The Rodopska nosia

* **Sukman** — a dark, near-black, heavy, sleeveless pinafore dress over the
  chemise; ankle-length and grounded.
* **Riza** — the white chemise beneath, with embroidered sleeves showing.
* **Prestilka** (woven apron) — earthy Rhodope reds and blacks in geometric
  stripes; the Rhodopes are known in-world (as in life) for their woven wool
  textiles.
* **Woven belt** and **headscarf** — hair covered and neat; little movement.

[[motion]]
=== Motion encodes personality

Both girls share a single procedural animation rig; a per-character `verve`
value scales how energetically the body reacts to motion. Anya's is `1.0`.
**Donna's is `0.42`.**

[cols="1,2", options="header"]
|===
| Reactive element | Donna (low verve)

| Body lean
| slight

| Walk bounce
| planted

| Jump height
| low, heavy

| Skirt
| heavy, damped

| Headscarf
| barely moves

| Hem sway
| subdued

| Reaction speed
| measured
|===

The design's own framing is worth keeping in mind: this is a schematic,
game-scale *evocation* of the embroidery and costume detail, not a literal
reproduction — a higher-fidelity portrait pass is explicitly a later art
task. What is fixed now is the *behaviour*: Donna's whole body reads as
grounded, before a single word of dialogue is spoken.

== Two Lands, One Heart

Slavia is not just geography. It is memory, medicine, folklore, and moral
choice — a world where places remember what happened in them, where every act
of courage, care, fear, resentment, trust, or betrayal leaves a mark on the
land. Donna and Anya do not simply travel through it. They are read by it,
healed by it, tested by it — and eventually, the design intends, they learn
to heal it in return.

=== Why the Rift chose both of them

The design's deepest answer to "why these two girls" is metaphysical, not
coincidental:

[quote, docs/design/01-world-principle.md]
____
Slavia is a world built on balance, and Anya and Donna are two halves of one
guardian spirit: Anya — the spirit's impulse. Donna — the spirit's structure.
Together — the spirit's harmony.
____

The Rift did not tear at them because they happened to be nearby. It tore at
them *because* they are the one whole thing capable of being split. Their
gift — attunement — belongs to them alone, in-world, for exactly this reason:
it is not two gifted girls who happen to complement each other, it is one
nature, split in two. The guardian spirit itself is glimpsed later in the
design (Zone E of the planned five-zone map) and gathered piece by piece
before that, through collectible Guardian Spirit Fragments — none of this is
built yet.

=== How she sees Anya

Where Anya has always felt misunderstood, Donna is canonically the first
person who doesn't try to slow her down. Donna's instinct instead is to
**anchor** her — not restrain her, not correct her, *anchor* her, the way she
anchors everything else she touches. The design notes that Anya trusts Donna
more than she admits — a trust Donna, in turn, has never had modelled back at
her: someone whose steadiness is *for* her, not extracted from her by
circumstance.

The two can also **sense each other across the Rift itself** — not merely
that the world tilts when one of them is missing, but a direct awareness that
survives the barrier between them. This is the mechanical seed for future
rescue missions, echo puzzles, and split-path levels: the world can separate
their bodies, but never fully their bond.

=== What they share, underneath the contrast

Their differences — order/logic/stability against chaos/impulse/motion — are
the engine of the game. But underneath the contrast, the design is careful to
name what actually makes them the chosen pair:

* **Courage** — both push forward even when afraid.
* **Loyalty** — neither abandons the other; the emotional spine of the story.
* **Curiosity** — both want to understand Slavia, even by opposite routes.
* **Resilience** — they keep going even when the Rift reacts violently.

== Her Side of Slavia

Everything in this chapter is **design canon for later** — none of it is
built in the current Zone A prototype, and several items are marked, in the
source documents themselves, as still open to the owner's final word. It is
included here because it is the clearest picture that exists of where Donna's
story is *headed*.

=== A possible path

The design's parking-lot notes sketch three tentative class leanings for
Donna — **Pillar, Architect, Guardian** — introduced, in-world, by an
apothecary figure who "diagnoses a leaning and offers a path," asking the
same question the whole game turns on: _"What do you become when the world
breaks?"_ No path has been chosen; this is a menu of possibilities, not a
commitment.

=== Spirits of her heritage

The Bulgarian/Rhodope side of the world's spirit roster (candidate figures,
not yet confirmed by the owner) includes the **samodiva / vila** (a
forest-mountain nymph), the **stopan** (a house spirit — the home hearth), and
unnamed gorski (forest) spirits. Like supernatural animals, a spirit's
alignment is never shown as an obvious tell (no glow, no halo) — it is
revealed only through behaviour, and the girls default to **innocent until
proven guilty**, a naivety the design treats as part of their character, not
a gap.

=== Flora and healing

If flora affinity follows the same taxis rule as animals — the design's stated
intent, though unimplemented — Donna would still and root growth rather than
quicken it: closing buds, deepening roots, calming a thrashing plant,
retracting overgrowth. The purest restorative in Slavia, deliberately kept
small and fixed rather than built into a sprawling recipe system, is the
shared Bulgarian/Ukrainian staple: **yogurt and honey**.

=== What she wears, as she grows

The clothing system's second, still-conceptual layer — **pattern weaving** —
would let Donna weave sacred-geometry motifs from the **Kanatitsa / Elbetitsa**
line (Bulgarian folk-embroidery traditions) into her sukman as she develops.
The design is emphatic that these patterns are never a "moveset of triggered
special moves" — no cooldowns, no combos. They are passive, ambient, and
above all **social**: a pattern is a blueprint of who you are, and the world
reads it. _"You have THAT pattern? Then of course you are welcome here."_

== Fan Corner

**Where her design comes from.** The design's own touchstones document names
_Hunter × Hunter_'s Nen/Zen aura system as the mechanical north star for
Donna's (and Anya's) gift — a rigorous, rule-based system that reveals
character rather than escalating into raw power, deliberately built *without*
the shōnen "everything becomes an ultimate" trap. It also names _Axis Powers
Hetalia_ as the touchstone for treating Donna as a genuine
**personification of a land** — Donna *is* Rhodope Bulgaria, the way that
show's characters *are* their countries — while explicitly leaving Hetalia's
comedy register behind in favour of something "mythic and earnest."

**A line worth knowing.** The design's one-sentence pitch for the whole game
doubles as the tightest possible description of what Donna is *for*:

[quote, docs/design/18-touchstones-and-positioning.md]
A folkloric Brothers-meets-Ōkami with a Trine-like switch between two girls —
one who stirs, one who stills — and Nen-like systematic depth, set in a
Slavic world that reads who you are.

**Legibility, in-world.** A later design layer called _manpu_ (manga-style
visual shorthand — swirls, sweat-drops, sleep-marks) is proposed to make the
world's hidden state readable at a glance. One sketched example belongs
entirely to Donna: she settles a sheepdog until it visibly falls asleep
(rendered with a manga "Zzz" mark), lowering its detection and opening a
stealth route. Not built; a strong hint of what "settling" will eventually
*feel* like to use.

== Open Questions for Further Development

These are drawn directly from what the design canon itself leaves
unresolved — not invented gaps, but the actual open questions a next
designer, writer, or wiki contributor would need to answer.

=== About Donna herself

. What does Donna actually look like, beyond her costume and hair colour? No
  age, height, build, eye colour, or skin tone is specified anywhere in the
  canon.
. What is Donna's surname, or her family's names? Her grandmother (the
  kukeri mask-carver) and uncle (the bridge-builder) are described only by
  role, never named.
. What, specifically, was the "collapsed walkway" she was repairing on the
  day of the Tear — where was it, and why had it collapsed?
. How does Donna actually *talk*? Only one line of dialogue exists for her in
  the whole corpus (&#8220;Why didn&#8217;t I just stabilise the bridge, Anya?&#8221;). Is she
  formal, wry, terse, warm-but-guarded?
. How did Donna and Anya first meet — before the Rift, or only after?

=== About her powers and path

. Which of her three sketched class paths — Pillar, Architect, or Guardian —
  is she meant to lean toward, or is the choice left entirely to the player?
. The imported (non-canon, flagged "to refine") material suggests Donna's
  anchoring, applied too rigidly, can *shear* things rather than protect
  them — is that tension something the finished story explores, or was it a
  discarded idea?
. Does Donna ever learn to ask for help — the flaw the design explicitly
  names for her — and if so, in which Chronicle?

=== About her world

. Which of the candidate Bulgarian/Rhodope spirits (samodiva, stopan, other
  gorski spirits) are confirmed canon, and do any of them appear in
  Chronicle I specifically, or only later?
. Do plants have will of their own, the way supernatural animals do, or only
  the rare supernatural ones?
. Is Chronicle I's tone meant to be calm and folkloric (as Zone A currently
  plays) or "surreal, dangerous, fast-paced" (as an earlier structure
  document describes it)? The design itself flags this as unresolved.
. Is the eventual game switch-only between the two girls, or genuinely
  co-operative/simultaneous? Both are recorded in different documents as
  live possibilities.
