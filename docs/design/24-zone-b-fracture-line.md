# Zone B — Fracture Line (first full draft)

## What this is

Zone B's story, developed from its single canon line in
`08-level1-five-zone-map.md` ("The Rift ruptures for real; the girls are
forced apart") into a beat-by-beat narrative design. **Status: first full
draft for owner review — nothing here is settled canon.** It follows the
pattern of `23-level-scope-and-pacing.md`: honest about what it resolves,
explicit about what it leaves open, and written so the owner can answer the
open questions conversationally rather than re-derive the whole zone.

Zone B follows **after all of A1–A5** (`23-level-scope-and-pacing.md`), not
after the old 7-beat prototype. That matters twice over: the player arriving
in Zone B has had five segments of togetherness, mastery, and belonging —
and A1's closing beat already spent the "marauder hunts them at the bridge"
idea (`06-attunement-and-modifiers.md`, *Zone A climax idea*) as a
**triumph**: Anya agitated the crossing and repelled the threat. Zone B must
not replay that beat with the outcome flipped. Whatever separates the girls
here has to be a different *kind* of thing, not a stronger marauder.

## Tone — provisional, pending an owner ruling

**This draft is written in the calm, dread-building Kishōtenketsu register,
not the "surreal, dangerous, fast-paced" register.** That is a provisional
choice, not a resolution: `22-narrative-structure-per-chronicle.md` (open
question 1) records the unresolved conflict between `13`'s tone line for
Chronicle I and the Kishōtenketsu blueprint, notes that the built Zone A
already backs the calm reading, and leaves the ruling to the owner. The
owner has authorized drafting Zone B under the calm reading *for now*. If
the ruling later goes the other way, this zone's pacing (not its underlying
mechanism) would need redrafting.

In Kishōtenketsu terms, Zone B is Level 1's ***Ten*** — and per `22`, the Ten
"need not be violent: it can be a paradigm-shifting realization, or a deep
architectural change in the state of the world." That is exactly what this
draft does: the rupture is not an attack. It is the world's grammar turning
inside out, quietly, underneath two girls who have just learned to trust it.

## The inciting mechanism — resolved (as a proposal)

Prior canon floated three candidates for what forces the separation:

1. **A marauder who succeeds** (`06`) — *spent.* A1 used the marauder and
   the girls won. Escalating to "a marauder, but worse" would make A1's
   triumph retroactively hollow and make Zone B a repeat.
2. **Collapsing terrain** (`12-core-loop-and-opening-level.md` §2,
   `14-chronicle-i-rift-between-nations.md`) — Anya outruns collapse, Donna
   stabilizes falling platforms. Strong *traversal* material; weak as a
   *reason*. Terrain alone is weather — it doesn't answer "why them, why
   now, why apart."
3. **A Rift-native abduction** (`09-anya-and-donna-backstories.md`, `14`) —
   the Rift *took* each girl once already, deliberately ("It saw her chaos.
   It needed her chaos."). Strong as intent; but a literal snatch-and-grab
   is a violent Ten, and it takes the separation *away* from the girls —
   they'd be victims of a cutscene, not participants in a tragedy.

**This draft blends 2 and 3: the terrain is the body, the abduction is the
intent — and the girls' own loyalty is the trigger.** The Rift does not send
a monster and does not reach out a hand. It opens a seam along the border
itself — the line between forest and mountain that Zone A's bridge stitched
together — and it opens it *along the exact fault in the girls' grammar*:

- Donna cannot settle the seam, because the seam is not agitated. It is the
  Rift's own order — a stillness deeper than hers.
- Anya cannot stir it shut, because stirring is her only verb and a wound
  does not close by being agitated.
- Only Donna can stabilise; only Anya can agitate; **neither can do the
  other's act** (`06`, *Opposition as a mechanic*). The Rift splits the
  world along precisely that line. The co-op grammar that solved every
  puzzle in A1–A5 has a hole in it, and the Rift *is* the hole.

And the final turn of the knife: at the crux, each girl gets one clear
chance to save herself — and each spends it on the other instead. Canon says
"neither abandons the other; the emotional spine of the story" (`09`). Zone
B makes that spine the mechanism of the separation. The Rift needed them
apart, and the only lever that could part such loyalty is the loyalty
itself.

This is why it doesn't feel like A1 again: A1's threat was external, human,
and beaten *by* the grammar working. Zone B's rupture is internal to the
world, deliberate, and succeeds *because* the grammar — and the girls —
work exactly as they should.

## The story, beat by beat

Movement names below are narrative structure, not committed segment/room
breakdown — Zone B's spatial shape and length are open questions (see
below), per `23`'s answered rulings (rooms, not corridors; length is a
per-zone call).

### B-i. The morning after (false Ketsu)

Zone B opens in the quiet after A5 — the world *changed but structurally
balanced*, which is exactly what a Kishōtenketsu ending is supposed to feel
like. The player is meant to half-believe the level is winding down.

Dawn on the overlook side of the ridge. Mist in the birches, woodsmoke from
somewhere Bulgarian. The girls are walking off the adrenaline of A5,
trading the kind of talk that only happens after something hard is over —
Anya replaying her best moments out loud, Donna quietly redistributing the
weight of both packs while Anya isn't looking. Donna has yogurt; Anya has
honey on her fingers and, somehow, in her hair.

The wrongness starts smaller than the player's attention. A bird Anya stirs
takes one beat too long to rise. A stream Donna settles goes still — and
then *stays* still, glassy, longer than settling should hold. Nothing the
game flags. The manpu are just slightly late.

> **Anya:** "Did you see that jay? It looked at me like it was deciding
> whether to bother."
> **Donna:** "It's early. Even birds are allowed to be tired."

### B-ii. The wrong answers

The grammar begins to mirror. This is the dread movement, and it should be
played, not narrated: the player *does* the discovering, through the same
stir/settle verbs they've trusted for five segments.

- Anya stirs a hare — and it settles, folding down into the grass like a
  stone. Wrong girl's answer.
- Donna settles a rope-and-plank span — and it shivers, one slow buck, as
  if remembering A1's bridge from the wrong side.
- The shrine stone from Zone A's twin appears here in passing — *"Two
  lands, one heart"* — but the embroidered votive cloths tied to it have
  their patterns running mirror-reversed, as though seen from inside the
  fabric.

Neither girl says the obvious thing for a long time, each protecting the
other from it — Anya by joking faster, Donna by finding practical reasons
("the ground here is iron-rich; it confuses things"). The world has been
*reading* them since the beginning (`01-world-principle.md`); now something
else is reading them, through the world, and getting the answers backwards
— or getting them *right*, for a purpose the girls can't see.

The animals do not flee. That would be warning. They *withdraw* — the
forest politely excusing itself, a room emptying before an argument.

### B-iii. The seam

They crest the last ridge and see it: where Zone A's fracture-prelude
tremor ran, there is now a **seam** — a hairline of light stitched along
the border between the lands, running through the valley like embroidery
pulled taut from underneath. Vyshyvanka geometry on the forest side of the
light; kukeri spirals on the mountain side; the two patterns meeting at the
seam and *not joining*. The light is not fire and not sky — it is the color
of the gap between the girls when they stand apart.

Up close, the seam hums at two pitches. Anya hears the high one in her
teeth. Donna feels the low one in her heels. Each girl assumes the other is
hearing what she hears. (This is A5's theory-of-mind lesson, inverted and
weaponized: acting on what you *believe* the other knows.)

They try the grammar on it, because that is who they are:

- **Donna settles the seam.** Nothing. Her stillness pours into it and is
  simply *absorbed* — the seam is already more ordered than she is. For the
  first time in the game, Donna's calm meets something calmer, and it
  frightens her the way nothing loud ever has.
- **Anya stirs the seam.** The light brightens — not resisting her,
  *feeding*. Her chaos meets something that wants chaos, and drinks it.

> **Donna:** "Don't. It's — it likes it."
> **Anya:** "Everything likes me. That's never been a good sign either."

This is the paradigm-shift beat: the player learns, hands-on, that the
opposition mechanic has an edge, and they are standing on it.

### B-iv. The fold

The rupture, when it comes, is quiet. No roar. The valley *folds like
paper* — the exact phrase of Donna's original fall (`09`) — the seam
widening not by shattering but by the two lands letting go of each other,
the way a held breath is finally let out.

Traversal here is the `12`/`14` escape material, placed where `14` says it
belongs — in Zone B, not Zone A: **Anya outruns the widening fold; Donna
stabilizes what is falling.** Birch trunks tip in slow motion and hang,
suspended in Donna's stillness, long enough to run across. Mountain ledges
shear and Anya's momentum carries her over gaps that did not exist a
heartbeat ago. The player switches — or the girls act in sequence — each
doing only her own half, the halves interleaving perfectly. The grammar
works flawlessly, all the way to the moment it can't.

**The crux.** One span left — a fallen birch bridging the fold, the last
stitch holding the two lands together, an unmistakable echo of A1's bridge.
The fold is widening from both ends. There is time for one act each:

- Donna can settle the birch — hold it steady long enough for *Anya* to
  cross to the mountain side, to her.
- Anya can stir the rockfall breaking loose above the far end — fling it
  wide of *Donna's* footing, keep her anchor-ground alive.

Each girl looks across the span, reads the other exactly right — five
segments of learning each other guarantee it — and each spends her one act
on the other. Donna steadies the ground under Anya's feet instead of the
crossing. Anya scatters the rockfall away from Donna instead of clearing
her own path. Both succeed. Both are safe.

And the birch, held by no one, goes down into the light without a sound.

They did not fail for lack of skill, or speed, or love. They failed
*because* of the love — and the seam knew they would. It saw her chaos. It
saw her order. It needed them **apart**.

> **Anya:** "Donna — jump, I'll stir the air, I'll — I can—"
> **Donna:** "You can't. Not that. Neither of us can do that."
>
> It is the first time Donna has ever said *can't* about herself, and she
> says it about both of them.

### B-v. Two shores

The fold settles into a **chasm** — wide, quiet, floored with the seam's
light far below. The Rift has sorted them each onto her own land: Anya on
the Ukrainian forest side, Donna on the Bulgarian mountain side (a choice,
not a given — see open questions). No music. Wind, and the two pitches of
the hum, now separated: Anya's shore hums high, Donna's low.

They can see each other. They will keep seeing each other — that is the
load-bearing geometry of what comes next (`08`: in Zones C and D "the
other is visible across the chasm throughout"). Voices don't carry across.
The bond does: they **sense** each other directly (`09`, *The pairing*) —
each girl knows, without hearing, that the other is standing at the edge
refusing to move first.

The emotional register to land: not panic, and not despair. A held, quiet
grief — and then, from each of them in her own idiom, resolve. Anya presses
her palm flat to the air as if the chasm were a window. Donna, precisely,
does the same, and neither can see the other's hand clearly at this
distance, and both know the other is doing it.

Around them, the tilt begins (`06`, *Separation tilts the world*): behind
Anya, her forest is already too bright, leaves trembling with no wind —
the first breath of Zone C's over-agitation. Behind Donna, the mountain has
gone heavy and exact, brittle as first frost — Zone D's over-order
arriving. Each girl turns from the edge into a world that is now *too much
her*, with the half that balanced her standing visible, and unreachable, on
the far shore.

> **Anya** (to herself, moving): "Okay. Okay. Forward is still a
> direction."
> **Donna** (to herself, still): "Hold. Hold everything. Then go and get
> her."

Zone B ends there. Zones C and D pick each girl up mid-stride.

## Handoff into Zones C/D — what this ending must guarantee

Per `08-level1-five-zone-map.md`, the next zones require: each girl alone;
her world tilted toward her own excess; the other girl visible across the
chasm throughout; and (eventually) reunion at the Rift Node Chamber in Zone
E. This draft's ending is built backwards from those requirements:

- **Geometry.** The chasm runs the length of both solo zones — Zone B ends
  with the girls on opposite shores of the same cut, not scattered to
  arbitrary places. C and D are the two banks of one wound.
- **World state.** The tilt is *shown starting* in B-v, so C and D open in
  an already-explicable world rather than a new arbitrary one.
- **Emotional state.** Neither girl is broken. Anya converts grief into
  motion; Donna converts it into purpose. Each enters her solo zone doing
  the thing she has always done — which is exactly what her tilted world
  will now punish and teach her about (`14`'s Personality Zones: puzzles
  that punish hesitation / puzzles that punish rushing).
- **The bond as mechanic.** The cross-chasm sense (`09`) is established
  on-screen in B-v, so C/D can use sightlines and sensed-presence beats
  (each girl seeing the other's distant progress) without introducing
  anything new.

## Open questions for the owner

In the manner of `23`'s open-questions section — these need answers before
Zone B is built; the draft above should be read as pending all of them.

1. **Chronicle I's tone (the standing one).** This draft assumes the calm /
   Kishōtenketsu reading, per the owner's provisional authorization — but
   the underlying conflict (`13`'s "surreal, dangerous, fast-paced" vs. the
   Kishōtenketsu blueprint) is **still open**: see
   `22-narrative-structure-per-chronicle.md`, open question 1. Not resolved
   here, and deliberately not resolvable here. If the ruling goes fast/
   dangerous, B-i through B-iii compress hard and B-iv expands.
2. **Is the crux playable or authored?** B-iv's loyalty trap is written as
   an inevitable outcome. Under ADR-0005 (fail-forward, no rollback —
   `22`), what happens if a player *tries* to defect — sprints the birch
   with Anya instead of saving Donna's ground? Options: (a) the grammar
   genuinely forbids it (the crossing physically requires the act she's
   forgoing — separation is systemic, not scripted); (b) the player can
   try, and failing costs something durable that carries into C/D. (a) is
   cleaner; (b) is braver. Owner call.
3. **Which girl on which shore?** This draft sorts each girl onto her own
   land (Anya → forest, over-agitated; Donna → mountain, over-ordered),
   reading the tilt as "her own nature, unbalanced." The inverse — each
   stranded in the *other's* land — is also defensible (estrangement
   instead of excess) and `08` doesn't rule on geography. Owner call; it
   shapes all of C and D's art direction.
4. **The marauder thread — closed or dormant?** A1 repelled the marauder;
   this draft deliberately does not reuse them. Do they reappear at all —
   e.g. glimpsed on one shore during C/D (a human problem in an inhuman
   time), or witnessed watching the fold from a ridge in B-iv? Or is that
   thread fully spent at A1?
5. **How legible is the Rift's intent in Zone B itself?** This draft lets
   the player *infer* deliberateness ("it needed them apart") but never
   states it. Is that reveal held for Zone E / later in Chronicle I ("the
   Rift is calling them," `13`), or should Zone B say it out loud?
6. **Zone B's size and room count.** Per `23`'s answered questions, zones
   vary in length (majority long, minority short) and are built as
   connected rooms. Is Zone B a *long* zone (the dread movement B-ii wants
   room to breathe) or a deliberately short, terrible one? And does the
   B-i…B-v structure map to rooms, to A-style internal segments with save
   points, or to something else?
7. **The A3 artefact across the split.** A5 *uses* the artefact
   (`23`); nothing says whether it is spent. If it persists, which girl
   carries it across the separation matters for C/D/E design — or does the
   fold split *it* too?
8. **Kishōtenketsu at level scale.** This draft treats Zone B as Level 1's
   *Ten*, which quietly endorses the fractal reading flagged in `22` (open
   question 5: A calm → B rupture → C/D consequences → E reunion). If the
   shape is meant to be fractal, that should be stated deliberately;
   flagged again here rather than assumed.
